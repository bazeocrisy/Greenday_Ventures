For Owners

Questions worth asking any buyer.

The right buyer should be willing to answer difficult questions before asking an owner to make a life-changing decision. These are worth asking of anyone who approaches you, including Greenday Venture.

Owners of well-run trade businesses get approached constantly. Most of those approaches lead nowhere, and a few lead somewhere an owner later regrets. The difference usually comes down to what was asked at the beginning.

Take this list to any conversation. Write the answers down. A buyer who is comfortable with careful questions is telling you something useful, and so is one who is not.

01

Ownership Intentions

Start here. Everything else follows from what the buyer actually intends to do with the company.

  • How long do you intend to own the business?
  • Are you acquiring the company directly, or on behalf of another investor?
  • Who will make major operating decisions after closing?
  • What could cause you to sell the company later?

02

Employees and Culture

Your team will feel the transition before your customers do.

  • What is your approach to the employees already here?
  • How do you evaluate leadership and key personnel?
  • Are compensation, benefits and roles expected to change?
  • How and when will employees learn about the transaction?

03

Business Identity

The name, the reputation and the way the work gets done are assets, not details.

  • Do you intend to keep the company name?
  • Will the company continue to operate locally?
  • What changes do you anticipate to branding, pricing or customer service?
  • How do you intend to protect the reputation the seller built?

04

Seller Involvement

Be specific about this early. Vague answers here cause the most friction later.

  • What role do you expect the seller to have after closing?
  • How long is the transition period, and what does it involve?
  • Will the seller remain responsible for revenue or day-to-day operations?
  • What happens if the seller wants to leave earlier than expected?

05

Financial and Transaction Structure

Ask these before you are emotionally committed to a deal.

  • How will the acquisition be financed?
  • Is any portion of the purchase price contingent on future performance?
  • What working-capital expectations will apply at closing?
  • What conditions could change the price between agreement and closing?

06

Process and Confidentiality

Your information is valuable whether or not a transaction happens.

  • Who exactly will have access to confidential information?
  • What happens to the seller's information if the deal does not close?
  • How will customer and employee confidentiality be handled?
  • Who pays for legal, accounting and diligence expenses?

07

References and Experience

A buyer's history, including the parts that did not go well, tells you more than their pitch.

  • Have you completed similar transactions?
  • Can I speak with former sellers, operators or professional advisors?
  • What did you learn from transactions that did not proceed?
  • Who will actually support the business after closing?

One Last Thing

A trustworthy buyer is not threatened by careful questions.

If asking these makes a conversation uncomfortable, that is information worth having early rather than late.
