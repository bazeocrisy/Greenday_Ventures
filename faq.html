Owner Questions

Questions business owners often ask.

Selling or transitioning a business raises personal, financial and operational questions. Those questions are welcome early, including when an owner is still years away from a decision.

Do I need to be ready to sell before contacting Greenday Venture?

No. Some of the most useful conversations happen with owners who are months or years away from a decision, or who are simply trying to understand what their options look like.

An early conversation costs you nothing and commits you to nothing. It gives both sides a chance to understand goals, timing and whether there is any fit at all. If the answer turns out to be no, you will still know more about your own options than you did before.

What happens during the first conversation?

It is an introduction, not a negotiation. Expect a conversation about the company's history, the work it does, how involved you are day to day, what you are hoping happens next, and what your timing looks like.

You decide how much to share. Nobody is asked for financial statements, customer lists or tax records in a first conversation, and there is no expectation that the discussion continues.

Is the first conversation confidential?

Greenday Venture's intention is to handle every early discussion discreetly and to avoid disclosing an owner's interest to employees, customers or competitors.

An informal conversation is not a legal protection, and should not be treated as one. Before any sensitive information changes hands, both sides should put an appropriate confidentiality agreement in place and have their own advisors review it. Ask any buyer, including this one, exactly who would have access to your information and what happens to it if a transaction does not proceed.

What happens to my employees?

In a home-service business the team is usually where much of the value actually sits. The technicians know the customers, the dispatcher knows the routes, and the field supervisor knows which jobs need a second pair of hands.

Greenday Venture's approach is to understand how a team works, what the roles are and what people are paid before forming any view about change. No buyer can responsibly promise in advance that every role and every arrangement stays exactly as it is, because that depends on understanding the business first. What can be said is that dismantling a working team is not the plan, and that questions about your people are welcome early rather than late.

Will the company keep its name?

Often the name on the truck is the reason the phone rings. Where a company's name, reputation and local identity carry real value, there is a strong argument for keeping them.

Whether that is the right answer depends on the company, the market, how customers find you and what the transition plan looks like. It is a decision to make together with an owner rather than a promise to make before understanding the business.

Will I need to stay after the sale?

That depends entirely on what you want. Seller involvement can range from a short handover of a few weeks, to several months of introductions and training, to a longer advisory or operating role for an owner who is not ready to step away.

The right structure comes out of the conversation. Some owners want to be finished on closing day; others want to keep doing the parts of the job they enjoy without carrying the administrative weight.

What if I am not ready to retire immediately?

Not every transition is a retirement. Greenday Venture is open to structures that let an owner stay involved for a defined period, reduce their hours gradually, or hand over responsibility in stages.

Each situation is structured individually, and the timeline should follow what the owner and the business actually need rather than an arbitrary schedule.

What kinds of companies does Greenday Venture consider?

Established home-service businesses with a real operating history, loyal repeat customers, an experienced team and a good reputation in their service area.

Current sectors of interest are plumbing, HVAC, electrical, landscaping, restoration and other specialty trades, with a geographic focus on Metro Atlanta and the Southeast. Financial requirements are not published, because figures on a web page tend to rule out good conversations before they start.

Does Greenday Venture work with business brokers and advisors?

Yes. Opportunities represented by business brokers, M&A advisors, CPAs and attorneys are welcome, subject to fit and straightforward professional communication.

Existing representation is respected, and there is no interest in going around an advisor to reach their client. There is more detail on the brokers and advisors page.

What information should I prepare?

For a first conversation, very little. A general sense of what services you offer, where you operate, how long you have been in business, roughly how many people work there, how you get customers, how involved you are day to day, and what you would like the next few years to look like.

Detailed financials, tax returns and customer information are not needed early, and should not be sent by email to anyone before appropriate agreements are in place.

What happens if we decide not to move forward?

Most conversations do not lead to a transaction, and that is a normal outcome rather than a failure.

If either side concludes there is not a fit, the discussion ends there. The intention is that a conversation is still worth having: many owners come away with a clearer sense of their timing, what a buyer will ask, and what they want to protect.

How long does an acquisition process take?

It varies, and a reliable answer depends on knowing the business first.

Timing depends on how prepared the company's records are, the complexity of the transaction, financing, legal and tax review, and how much transition planning the owner wants. Some owners also deliberately move slowly because a quiet, unhurried process is easier on the business.
