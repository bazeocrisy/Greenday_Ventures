Our Process

Unhurried, discreet, and yours to stop at any point.

Most owners want to understand how a conversation works before they have one. This is the general shape of it.

  1. Confidential introduction

    You reach out directly, or a broker, accountant, or attorney makes an introduction. Your interest is approached discreetly, with care taken not to disclose it to employees, customers, or competitors.

  2. Initial conversation

    A conversation about the company, your goals, and your timing. You decide what to share. Many owners use this stage simply to learn what a transition could look like, with no intention of moving quickly.

  3. Mutual-fit review

    Both sides consider whether the fit is worth exploring further. If it is not, the conversation ends there, and what was discussed is intended to remain private.

  4. Further evaluation, when appropriate

    If both sides want to continue, the discussion becomes more specific. Information is requested in stages, and only what is needed at each stage. The pace is set with the business in mind so that day-to-day operations stay steady.

  5. Transition planning, when both parties choose to proceed

    Planning covers the practical questions: how and when the team is told, what your role looks like afterward, and how continuity is maintained for customers. Terms, documentation, and closing are handled with each side's professional advisors.

Every situation is different. This page is a general overview of how conversations typically begin, not a binding acquisition process, an offer, or a description of the legal steps involved in a transaction. Nothing here should be relied on as legal, tax, or financial advice.
